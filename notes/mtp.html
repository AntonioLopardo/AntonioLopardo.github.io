Notes · Method note 01

Multi-token prediction

A normal language-model head predicts the next token. MTP adds a small extra head that predicts the token after that from the same hidden state. It costs almost nothing in parameters, it makes the training signal denser, and at inference it doubles as a draft model that lives inside the main model — which is why every 2026 frontier open model ships one.

Fig 1 · What is added at training time

The math in three lines

L  = L_main + λ · Σ_k L_MTP^(k)   // k = 1 for one extra token; DeepSeek-V3 uses k=1, Gloeckle et al. up to 4
h_t^(k) = Block_k( W_k · [ RMSNorm(h_t^(k−1)) ; RMSNorm(Emb(x_{t+k})) ] )   // h_t^(0) is the trunk output
p(x_{t+k+1} | ·) = softmax( W_out · h_t^(k) )   // W_out shared with the main head

λ is the only knob. DeepSeek-V3 uses 0.3 for the first 10T tokens and 0.1 after; Nemotron 3 Super uses 0.3 throughout SFT. The MTP block is one layer out of 60–90, so the parameter and FLOP overhead is a few percent.

Why it is kept

  • Denser supervision. Every position now yields two gradients instead of one, and the second one asks the representation to be usable two steps out — a mild pressure to “plan.” Gloeckle et al. report the effect grows with model size: for 13B, +12% on HumanEval and +17% on MBPP with four heads, and no gain (sometimes loss) at small sizes. DeepSeek-V3 reports consistent improvements across benchmarks from a single MTP module.
  • Free speculative decoding. At inference the MTP head is a draft model that shares the trunk: it proposes x_{t+2} for the price of one small block, and the main model verifies the proposal in the next forward pass. DeepSeek-V3: the second token is accepted 85–90% of the time across domains, giving about 1.8× tokens per second. No separate drafter to train, serve, or keep in sync.
  • It is inside the weights. A separate EAGLE-style drafter has to be retrained every time the main model changes; an MTP head is part of the checkpoint and can be re-healed with a short, cheap pass.

Pitfalls

  • RL breaks the draft. Any stage that moves the trunk — RLVR especially — lowers acceptance because the head was trained against the old trunk. Budget a healing pass after post-training, or the speedup quietly disappears in the deployed model.
  • Acceptance depends on the sampling distribution. A draft tuned for greedy decoding accepts less at temperature 1, and vice versa; Kimi K3's choice to optimize −log Σ min(p, q) at temperature 1 is a response to exactly this.
  • Only pays when decoding is memory-bound. Speculative decoding trades extra compute for fewer weight reads. At large serving batch sizes the main model is already compute-bound and the draft adds cost without saving time.
  • Small models may lose. Gloeckle et al. found MTP hurt some benchmarks below ~1B; the training-signal argument is a scale story.
  • Parallel vs sequential is not cosmetic. Independent heads predict x_{t+2} without knowing x_{t+1}, so their drafts are less coherent; the sequential module conditions on it, which is why the DeepSeek form is the one used for drafting.
