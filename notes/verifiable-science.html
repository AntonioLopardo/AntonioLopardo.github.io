Verifiable science data and environments

Ten papers on scalable, verifiable science data and environments for the Apertus 2 STEM climb. Nine are from the last twelve months; only MegaScience (July 2025) is older. Grouped by where the verifiable signal comes from.

The four for the slide: one executable environment, one simulator, one mined corpus, and the held-out target. Each in plain words first, then the numbers.

D3-GymExecutable science environment

  • In plain words: take a real scientific repo with its data, turn a step of its analysis into a task, and synthesise a script that checks the model's output. The model writes and runs code; the script grades.
  • 565 tasks from 239 real repositories: bioinformatics, computational chemistry, GIS, psychology
  • Verifier scripts synthesised plan-then-code, 87.5% agreement with human pass/fail verdicts
  • Rejection-sampled trajectories lift Qwen3-32B by +7.8 on ScienceAgentBench
  • The coding-heavy core: the verifier-synthesis step is reusable on Apertus-clean repos
CMU, LambdaApril 2026arxiv 2604.11805
codemodels 3B to 30B

Sim2ReasonSimulator-generated physics

  • In plain words: sample random physics scenes (masses, inclines, springs, initial velocities) as text, build each one in the MuJoCo engine, run it, and read the answer off the trajectory. Nobody annotates anything; the model only ever sees the text problem.
  • Three question types per scene: numeric (predict a quantity), reverse (recover a parameter from the outcome), symbolic (give the expression)
  • Trained on simulated data only: IPhO mechanics +5.4 at 32B, +7.5 at 3B; up to +17.9 on JEEBench
  • The clearest sim-to-real transfer result; the pattern to copy for chemistry and circuits

WildSciLiterature-mined QA

  • In plain words: feed peer-reviewed papers to an LLM, get multiple-choice questions out, filter them by refinement and model voting. Cheap and broad, no code involved.
  • 56K MCQ from Nature Communications papers, 9 disciplines, 26 subdomains
  • GRPO with MCQ reward on Qwen 1.5B and 3B: +7.3 average on GPQA-Aug, SuperGPQA, MMLU-Pro
  • Template for mining Swiss and EU open-access corpora; the non-coding counterweight in the mix

MAI-Thinking-1the one published STEM-expert recipe

  • Three specialists: STEM and competitive code, agentic coding and tool use, helpfulness and safety. Same RL recipe, different prompts and rewards. Consolidated by SFT on their traces, then a final RL stage.
  • STEM climb is "the longest of our three domain-specific RL training runs", single-turn, no tools. Every instance is (q, a) verified by SymPy or a judge, or (q, tests) for code.
  • Pipeline is the work: OCR and hierarchical parsing of PDFs, Q-A pairing across chapters and answer keys, taxonomy, PII and answer-leakage filters, MCQ and proofs rewritten to open-ended (MCQ can be guessed, proofs need a judge), 3× consensus, difficulty bins from pass rates of four solver tiers, blind grading to drop faulty ground truths.
  • Competitive code is a separate 160K-problem pipeline with tests, 17 languages. Dedup and decontamination in three stages against all reported benchmarks.
  • This is what "STEM expert" means in the Apertus plan. Scope it as this, minus code, and the deliverable is the verified prompt set with difficulty bins.

Nemotron 3 Ultrathe broadest definition

  • "General reasoning capabilities on a wide range of subjects including math, code, natural sciences, humanities, sociology, and tool use for these domains." Competitive coding is a separate teacher on top of it.
  • SFT seeds: Nemotron-Science-v1, a chemistry set, Multi-subject-RLVR, an internal set; easiest removed; 4 traces each (16 for hard) from DeepSeek-V4-Pro, judged by gpt-oss-120b. Held-out 3,000 prompts at pass rate 0.25 to 0.80.
  • After SFT, math, code and science were saturated, so RL ran on humanities and sociology prompts and "generalizes well, leading to significant improvements on all domains".
  • Teacher vs student: HLE 25.6 to 32.1, GPQA 85.0 to 88.5, IMOAnswerBench 84.5 to 92.5, Apex Shortlist 68.9 to 85.4. After MOPD the merged model kept only 17% of the HLE gain.
  • Two lessons: humanities prompts count as reasoning data, and the knowledge part of the gain does not survive the merge.

The restwhat the other reports actually say

  • Kimi K3: no STEM expert. Three experts, general, general agents, coding agents, each at three effort levels; STEM appears only as verifiable visual reasoning inside agentic environments.
  • DeepSeek-V4: "for each target domain, such as mathematics, coding, agent, and instruction following, a separate expert model is trained": SFT then GRPO, nothing on data or rewards; the math expert is named once more as the OPD teacher for math prompts. Formal math is a separate Lean 4 agentic setting.
  • Nemotron 3 Super: no experts at all, one policy over 21 environments; the finding is that joint training is stable and single-environment training regresses elsewhere.
  • Nemotron-Cascade 2: the "math teacher" in its MOPD is the SFT checkpoint itself, trained on 2.6M competition-math samples and 816K proofs; no math RL stage.
  • Qwen3, AceReason: single models with a math-and-code RL stage; Qwen3's 3,995-pair prompt set is the smallest published climb.
  • Consensus: math and the sciences, single-turn, verifiable, no tools; code and proofs are their own thing everywhere.
