Self-distillation, and the harness it quietly depends on

A model trained to match its own predictions under a richer condition — an older copy of itself, a prompt it will not have at test time, the feedback it just received, the tools it just called. No external teacher, a dense per-token signal, and a built-in anchor against forgetting. In 2026 it moved from a regularizer to a post-training stage. This note is about what it optimizes, why it forgets less than RL until suddenly it forgets more, and why the thing being distilled is usually not the model alone but the model plus its harness — so the two have to be versioned together.

The math in three lines

The objective. Sample from the student, score with the teacher, match per token. \(c\) is the privileged condition; the teacher is stop-gradient. \[ \mathcal{L}(\theta)=\mathbb{E}_{x}\;\mathbb{E}_{y\sim\pi_\theta(\cdot\mid x)}\;\sum_{t}\; D\!\left(\pi_{\bar\theta}(\cdot\mid x,c,y_{<t})\;\middle\|\;\pi_\theta(\cdot\mid x,y_{<t})\right) \] The teacher is what you choose to put in \(c\). Three instantiations cover almost everything published: \[ \pi_{\bar\theta}(\cdot\mid x,c)=\begin{cases}\pi_{\theta_{\text{old}}}(\cdot\mid x) & \text{previous release — the forgetting anchor (LwF)}\\[2pt] \pi_{\theta}(\cdot\mid \text{prompt},x) & \text{context you will not have at test time (context distillation)}\\[2pt] \pi_{\theta}(\cdot\mid x,y,\text{feedback}) & \text{the model reading its own result (SDPO)}\end{cases} \] Why it is gentler than RL, and why that can flip. The per-token target is a full distribution, not a scalar; the gradient is a projection onto a function the model already computes, so each step moves little. But when the teacher is the same moving weights, dense matching can chase its own artifacts — the loop is self-reinforcing. Denser ≠ Better measures exactly this: under continual post-training SDPO drifted more than GRPO in both parameter and response space, and the fix (StableSDPO) is to freeze or periodically restart the teacher. \[ \underbrace{\text{dense signal}}_{\text{fast in-domain}}\;+\;\underbrace{\text{teacher}=\text{student}}_{\text{no fixed point}}\;\Rightarrow\;\text{drift};\qquad \text{fix: } \bar\theta \leftarrow \theta_{k}\ \text{held for a window, or } \theta_{\text{old}}\ \text{throughout.} \]

Why it is kept

  • No external teacher. The 2026 frontier open models all distill, and the strong ones distill from themselves or their own larger sibling — there is no bigger model to call. Self-distillation is the version that scales past the biggest thing you have.
  • Dense where RL is sparse. A verifier gives one bit per rollout; the teacher gives a distribution per token. Thinking Machines' measurements put on-policy distillation at a fraction of RL's compute for the same reasoning gain, and it is the mechanism they name for continual learning and personalization: re-teach a specialized skill after a general update without losing the update.
  • It internalizes context. Context distillation (Snell, Klein, Zhong 2022) showed a model can absorb an instruction, a set of in-context examples, or a scratchpad into its weights by matching its own context-conditioned outputs. That is the same loss with \(c\) = the prompt — and it is why the harness question below is not separate from the method.
  • It is the oldest continual-learning trick there is. Learning without Forgetting (2016) is a KL to the previous network on new-task inputs. Every "anchor to the old policy" term since — including the KL-to-reference in RLHF — is a special case.
  • Feedback becomes a teacher. SDPO's move: condition the model on the compiler error, the failed test, or the next user turn, and distill that hindsight-informed distribution back into the un-conditioned policy. Rich feedback that RL would have to scalarize is used whole.

Fig 2 · The harness evolves too — version the pair, not the model

Why this framing matters for continual post-training. Most forgetting measurements hold the harness fixed and vary the weights. But in an agentic stack the harness is where a surprising amount of behaviour lives — the system prompt that names the tools, the template that marks tool outputs, the verifier that defines "correct". Change it and a model can lose a skill without a single weight moving. Context distillation is the tool for moving that behaviour into the weights before the harness changes; a KL to the old pair is the tool for keeping it there. Treating (model, harness) as the unit of versioning makes both steps explicit, and makes "does the next post-training benefit from the previous one" a well-posed question: the previous pair is the teacher.

Pitfalls

  • The teacher moves. When teacher and student share weights, dense matching has no fixed point and can amplify formatting quirks until the model collapses onto them. Hold the teacher for a window or pin it to the last release; measure response-space drift, not just loss.
  • Forgetting is not monotone in distance. Denser ≠ Better found the worst losses on tasks moderately related to the training domain, not on distant ones. A held-out suite that only checks far-away skills will miss it.
  • Long, variable reasoning traces distill badly. CoT distillation helped structured tool-use tasks and hurt math and science in the same study. Match the trace length to what the student can imitate, or distill outcomes rather than traces there.
  • Harness leak masquerades as forgetting. If a skill existed only under a system prompt or template that has since changed, no amount of weight-space anchoring recovers it. Evaluate the previous model under the new harness before blaming the update.
  • On-policy data alone is not enough. The continual-learning result is explicit on this: self-generated rollouts do not substitute for some replay of old inputs. Which old inputs to replay — and whether a distillation target beats a replayed label — is the open question, and the one closest to the classic continual-learning literature.
  • Consolidating experts is a different problem from anchoring one. Distilling several specialist checkpoints into one student has no single "previous self"; the teachers disagree. The number of experts, their overlap, and how to weight their distributions is not settled anywhere public.
