Notes · Method note 03

Latent communication between models

Two language models normally talk through text: the first decodes its state into tokens, the second re-encodes them. Latent communication skips the round trip. The sender's hidden states or KV cache go straight into the receiver through a small bridge, and both models stay frozen. Fifteen papers since 2024 do a version of this at 0.5B–14B; a startup, Mostik, now claims it from a 753B sender into a 4B receiver. The last tab takes the same design to diffusion: a block-diffusion head on a frozen AR trunk is the bridge with a diffusion receiver, and it is the fastest lossless decoder there is.

Fig 1 · The channel

The math in three lines

\[\text{text:}\quad m = \operatorname{decode}\!\big(h^{S}\big) \in V^{T},\qquad h^{R} = \operatorname{Enc}_{R}(m)\] // one token per step, at most \(\log_2 |V| \approx 17\) bits; \(h^{S}_{k}\) holds \(d\) reals per position and is discarded \[\text{latent:}\quad \tilde h = f_{\theta}\!\big(h^{S}_{k}\big),\qquad h^{R}_{j} \leftarrow h^{R}_{j} + \tilde h \quad\text{or}\quad \mathrm{KV}^{R}_{j} \leftarrow \big[\,f_{\theta}(\mathrm{KV}^{S}_{k})\,;\,\mathrm{KV}^{R}_{j}\big] \quad\text{or}\quad h^{R}_{j} \leftarrow h^{R}_{j} + \operatorname{Attn}\!\big(h^{R}_{j},\, f_{\theta}(h^{S})\big)\] // add (AC, SDE) · prepend to the cache (C2C, LatentMAS, KVComm, StateBridge) · cross-attend (Mixture of Thoughts, XBridge) \[\min_{\theta}\ \mathbb{E}_{(x,y)}\Big[-\log p_{R}\big(y \mid x,\ f_{\theta}(h^{S}(x))\big)\Big],\qquad \phi_{S},\ \phi_{R}\ \text{frozen}\] // training-free special cases: \(f = \mathrm{Id}\) when S and R are the same model (AC, LatentMAS); \(f = W^{\star} = \arg\min_{W^{\top}W = I}\lVert W H^{S} - H^{R}\rVert_F\) fitted on paired states (StateBridge)

\(\theta\) is the only knob, and it is small. Zero parameters when sender and receiver are the same model, about 1% of the combined pair in the Bicameral model, 3.8% of the receiver (264M) in XBridge. Everything else about the two models is untouched, which is the whole point: the bridge is retrained, not the models.

Why it is kept

  • The text is a summary of the state, and the state carries more. Hanna & Ameisen show Qwen-3 holds a representation of a planned word (“accountant”) several tokens before writing it, and that this is what produces the “an” in front of it; the effect grows from 0.6B to 14B. Gurnee et al. find a small, shifting set of concepts the model is poised to say but does not (the “J-space”), which it can hold silently while the visible output continues. Both are the interpretability results the communication papers, and Mostik's page, lean on.
  • It beats the text hand-off in every paper that measures it. Activation communication: +27% over text on coordination and reasoning tasks. Cache-to-Cache: 3–5 points over text. XBridge: better than text on all seven tasks for every model pair. StateBridge: best or tied on 22 of 26 model–task pairs, including against trained projectors.
  • No decoding of the message. The receiver never waits for the sender to generate its report token by token: 2× faster in C2C, 11× lower latency in XBridge, under a quarter of the compute in activation communication, up to 24× with Interlat's compression. The sender's prefill is still paid, see the pitfalls.
  • The asymmetric case is the useful one. A large model reads, a small model writes. That is LLM-to-SLM from 2024 (frozen large encoder, small fine-tuned decoder, up to 4× faster at 1–2 points cost), C2C's Qwen3-4B → Qwen3-0.6B pair, and the setup Mostik is selling.

Mostik against the literature

  • The design is Cache-to-Cache and XBridge's. Two frozen models, a small trained bridge, hidden states instead of tokens. The “a million numbers thrown away per token” argument is the activation-communication paper's opening motivation. The two interpretability citations are real and say what the page says they say.
  • What would be new: the scale of the sender (753B where the literature stops at 14B), the cross-family pair (GLM → Qwen; the survey lists cross-family bridges as rare), and a 4B receiver where C2C uses 0.6B. Size asymmetry at this ratio has not been published by anyone.
  • What is unverified: there is no paper, no bridge size, no benchmark names, and no named baseline behind “a mid-sized model”. The ≈10-point gain over a text hand-off sits in the C2C / XBridge band, so it is plausible. “Closes half the gap to the 753B model” is stronger than anything published and is the number to ask for.
  • The compute claim needs the prefill counted. The 753B sender still runs over the prompt every time. LLM-to-SLM accounts for this honestly: the saving is in decoding only, so “2.5× cheaper than a mid-sized model” is true only for long generations after short prompts, if at all.

Two readings of “AR to diffusion”

“Converting” can mean rewriting the AR weights into a diffusion LM, or keeping the AR model frozen and attaching a diffusion decoder that reads its latents. The bridge design maps onto the second cleanly, and that version already exists: it is DFlash, the fastest lossless speculative decoder published, and it is the MTP head widened from one token to a block.

Attach, don't convert

  • DFlash, ICML 2026. A lightweight block-diffusion drafter conditioned on context features from the target. Over 6× lossless speedup, up to 2.5× more than EAGLE-3, because one forward pass drafts the whole block where EAGLE-3 needs one per token.
  • DFlare, June 2026. Per-layer fusion: each draft layer attends to its own learnable combination of target layers, trained on 2.4M samples instead of DFlash's 800K. 5.5× on Qwen3-4B and Qwen3-8B, 3.9× on GPT-OSS-20B.
  • Why it sidesteps conversion. The trunk stays causal and frozen, so nothing is lost to the bidirectional-attention switch or to denoising at random mask rates. Only the head is trained, and the AR model verifies, so quality is exact by construction.
  • It inherits the bridges' open questions. The head is fit to the trunk's states and goes stale when the trunk is post-trained, MTP healing again. A learned gate could let the diffusion head emit easy spans unverified. RLVR on the head with the trunk frozen is untried.

  • The trend is “keep the causal prefix, grow the block”. Every 2025–26 method treats the AR model as block diffusion with block size 1 and widens it. The adaptation budget fell from 580B tokens to about 1B in a year, so knowledge transfer is no longer the bottleneck.
  • Alignment is already a conversion regularizer, in its simplest form only. Don't Retrain, Align uses the identity map, the same architecture, every layer. Untried: an affine or cross-size bridge (align a small diffusion model to a large AR model's states, the size-asymmetric transfer the survey on the Papers tab calls unstudied), selective layers, and the uniform-noise setting. Von Rütte et al. find uniform diffusion more parameter-heavy than masked, which suggests its representations sit further from AR's, so whether an AR teacher helps or hurts under GIDD-style noise is open.
  • A bridge alone converts nothing. It transports state between two working models; it does not change how either one generates. The hard part of conversion is the factorization, not the representation.

Verdict

  • Most promising: the attachment. A diffusion head on a frozen AR trunk is where the bridge design is cleanest and the wins are already lossless and large.
  • Next: alignment beyond the identity map. Cross-size, selective-layer, and non-masked-noise versions of Don't Retrain, Align are open and cheap to run.
  • Caveat. The speedups are small-batch, memory-bound numbers, the same regime where MTP pays. Diffusion LMs still do not beat AR plus speculative decoding per FLOP at scale, and parallel decoding has its own quality trade-offs (ParallelBench).

Pitfalls

  • Parameter-free is not assumption-free. Even the training-free methods choose a layer, rescale norms, and decide what position the injected states occupy. The survey's line: “parameter-free does not mean assumption-free.” Get any of the three wrong and the receiver reads noise.
  • Same backbone is the default. Most results are one model talking to a copy of itself. Cross-family needs training, one bridge per ordered pair unless a shared space is used (Mixture of Thoughts), and transfer to unseen pairs is open.
  • Post-training on either side breaks the bridge. The bridge was fit against two fixed spaces; RL or SFT on the receiver moves its residual stream and the alignment goes stale, the same way an MTP head loses acceptance after RLVR. Budget a re-fit.
  • It is a high-privilege channel. Nothing in the receiver checks what arrives; the survey flags modification, replay and malicious senders, and latent injection attacks on these systems have already been demonstrated.
  • The numbers across papers do not compare. Different backbones, prompts, context lengths and hardware; every “vs text” figure above is against that paper's own text baseline. Only the survey's taxonomy transfers, not the deltas.
  • Latent communication is not latent reasoning. Coconut-style continuous thoughts flow step to step inside one model; these methods flow agent to agent. Papers and product pages blur the two.
